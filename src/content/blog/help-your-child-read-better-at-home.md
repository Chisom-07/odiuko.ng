If your child struggles to read, you are not alone, and it is not your fault. Many Nigerian parents worry when their child skips words, reads slowly, or avoids books completely. The good news is that you do not need to be a teacher to help. Ten focused minutes a day at home can make a real difference.

1. Read together every day

Choose a quiet time, such as after dinner or before bed. Let your child read aloud while you listen. If they get stuck, give them a few seconds before helping, because that pause is where learning happens. Keep it short and calm. A relaxed 10 minutes beats a stressful hour.

2. Let your child choose the book

Children read more when they are curious. Let them pick stories about animals, football, superheroes, or Nigerian folktales. The goal at this stage is to build the habit, so it does not matter if the book looks “too easy.”

3. Break hard words into sounds

When your child meets a long word, show them how to split it into smaller parts. For example, “tomorrow” becomes “to-mor-row.” Say each part slowly, then blend them together. This helps with spelling too.

4. Ask questions as they read

After a page or paragraph, ask simple questions:

What just happened?
Who is the main character?
What do you think will happen next?

Reading is about understanding, not just saying the words correctly. If your child can retell the story in their own words, they are learning.

5. Make reading part of normal life

Point out words everywhere: shop signs, food labels, church bulletins, text messages. Ask your child to read the signboard on the road or the ingredients on a pack of noodles. Reading becomes useful, not just a school task.

6. Celebrate small wins

Praise effort, not perfection. Say “I like how you tried that word again” instead of “No, that’s wrong.” Children who feel safe will keep trying, and children who feel judged will hide.

What to do by class level
Basic 1-2: Focus on letter sounds, simple words, and short sentences.
Basic 3-4: Move to short stories, spelling patterns, and reading with expression.
Basic 5-6: Focus on comprehension, vocabulary, and reading for information.
When to ask for extra help

Sometimes home practice is not enough. Consider extra support if your child:

Is still reading below their class level after several months
Guesses words instead of sounding them out
Avoids reading or becomes upset when asked to read
Reads well but cannot explain what they read

Early support makes the biggest difference. Struggling readers can catch up, and the sooner they get help, the easier it is.
